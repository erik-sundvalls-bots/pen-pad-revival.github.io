Controls

  • Generated, Labels, and Clustering are switches beside the search box. Generated and Clustering are on at each start. Clustering off shows every ancestor as its own node. Siblings is a button in the side panel, off until you turn it on.
  • A dashed +n pill is a cluster of intermediate ancestors. Click the pill to expand it. Click the coloured background of an expanded cluster, not a node, to collapse that cluster again.
  • Zoom in, zoom out, 1:1 and fit sit at the upper right of the graph. Drag the background to pan. The wheel pans. Ctrl or ⌘ and the wheel zoom.
  • The side panel slides off to the right. The narrow strip keeps icon buttons: show the concept again, open this panel, show or hide the focus concept’s children, show or hide its siblings, ancestors only, and definition relationships. The same actions sit at the top of the open panel. The strip remembers whether it was open.
  • With a mouse, hover a node for its name without changing focus, and hover a collapsed cluster to read the hidden names. A click refocuses. On a touch screen the first tap is that preview and the second tap on the same node refocuses or expands the cluster. The preview closes if you tap elsewhere, pan or zoom, change focus, tap its close mark, or leave it for a few seconds. While Labels is off, that preview also writes the names onto the node’s immediate parents, children and drawn relationship targets, and lists the same names in the preview, so a covered or crowded dot can still be read.
  • The address of the focused concept is #concept=Name.
  • Relationships are off until you turn a kind on. They are the roles in the concept’s OWL definition, drawn as dashed edges in the colour of their property group. Every selected target is climbed by is-a to the root, and clustering folds the ancestors those targets share. The encircled i beside a heading explains how to read that section. A sanction in the same colour names a role that is allowed and is not drawn; a definition link is what this concept has. A nested filler such as hasConsequence (X which hasReversibility Reversible) is one edge to X, or to the generated which-class when that class is the filler. The nesting stays in the definition text.
